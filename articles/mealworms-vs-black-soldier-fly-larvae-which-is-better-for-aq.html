Mealworms vs. Black Soldier Fly Larvae: Which Is Better for Aquarium Fish?

Key Takeaways

  • Mealworms and black soldier fly larvae (BSFL) are the two most common dried insect treats for large aquarium fish and turtles, but they are different insects.
  • Typical dried BSFL contain about 40% protein, 28–32% fat, 7–10% fibre and 3% calcium. Typical dried mealworms contain about 50% protein, 25% fat, 7% fibre and very little calcium.
  • BSFL are the better source of calcium. Mealworms have a little more protein, which most fish already get from their staple food.
  • BSFL can be raised on organic waste and generally need less space and water, which makes them a more sustainable choice.

If you keep Arowana, Oscar, Flowerhorn, large cichlids or turtles, you know how quickly they come to the surface at treat time. An insect treat adds variety to the diet, and for shy or fussy feeders it can be a good way to build trust at feeding time.

Mealworms and black soldier fly larvae are the two names you will see most often. They look similar in the pack, but they are different insects, and their nutrition is different. So which is the better choice for your tank?


What Are They?

Black soldier fly larvae (BSFL) are the young stage of the black soldier fly. The adult fly does not bite or sting and is not a household pest. Like all insects it goes through egg, larva, pupa and adult stages. The larvae are harvested and dried while they are still larvae, and they are thick, short and ridged, with a tapered end.

BSFL are known for a very big appetite. In farms they turn organic waste into body mass quickly, and the time from hatching to harvest can be short, which keeps them affordable to raise.

Mealworms are the larvae of the darkling beetle. They are longer, thinner and smoother than BSFL, and a more even golden colour when dried. The beetle's life cycle is longer than the fly's, so mealworms generally take more time and space to produce.

Because the two look alike in a pack, buyers sometimes confuse them. Always check the ingredient line on the label, and if you are unsure, ask the seller for the species name.

Black Soldier Fly Larvae Nutrition vs. Mealworm Nutrition

Both insects eat a lot during their larval stage, but their nutrition is different. These are typical values for dried larvae:

Dried BSFLDried mealworm
Proteinabout 40%about 50%
Fatabout 28–32%about 25%
Fibreabout 7–10%about 7%
Calciumabout 3%very low

The numbers change with what the larvae were fed and how late they were harvested. Larvae harvested closer to the pupa stage store more fat, so two packs of the same insect can differ. Always use the figures printed on the pack you buy.

Protein: mealworms have more, but more is not always better. Large fish already get plenty of protein from their pellets, and a treat is not meant to be their main diet.

Fat: both are fairly high in fat, which gives energy but also means they should be a treat and not a daily meal.

Fibre: BSFL have a little more, which can help digestion.

BSFL Calcium vs. Mealworm Calcium

The biggest advantage BSFL have over mealworms is calcium. Dried BSFL carry a meaningful amount of it, and mealworms carry very little.

Calcium supports healthy bones and body structure, and in fish keeping it is a nutrient that many everyday foods do not provide much of. A treat that adds calcium is a nice change from foods that are mostly protein.

Calcium works together with phosphorus, so balance matters. A treat that is high in phosphorus but low in calcium is not a balanced source of both, and this is where BSFL do better than mealworms.

BSFL are a treat, not a replacement for a complete diet. Use them to add variety and extra calcium on top of a good staple food.

BSFL Farms vs. Mealworm Farms

What the larvae were fed has a direct effect on their nutrition, so how they are farmed matters.

Good BSFL and mealworm producers keep the larvae clean, feed them controlled diets and dry them properly. Poor producers can sell dusty, mouldy or badly dried product.

When you buy, look for:

  • A clear label that names the species and lists only one ingredient
  • Nutrition figures printed on the pack
  • Dry, clean pieces with no dust, mould or sour smell
  • An honest weight in grams

BSFL Sustainability vs. Mealworm Sustainability

BSFL are often considered the more sustainable of the two. They can be raised on organic waste that would otherwise be thrown away, and they convert it into protein quickly. In general they need less space and less water than mealworms for the same amount of protein.

Mealworms can also be raised on plant waste and grain by-products, but they usually take longer to grow and need more space.

Fish feeds that use insect protein also reduce the pressure on wild-caught fish used for fishmeal, so insect-based treats can be a more planet-friendly choice for fish keepers.

BSFL or Mealworms?

Both are fine treats, and they are not the same product:

  • Choose BSFL if you want a calcium-rich treat with a good balance of nutrients, or you want a more sustainable option.
  • Choose mealworms if you want a higher-protein treat and are happy with its lower calcium.
  • Rotate both if your fish enjoy variety, and keep either one to a few times a week.

For most keepers of large fish, dried BSFL are the better everyday treat because of their calcium.

Frequently Asked Questions

Are BSFL the same as mealworms?
No. They are different insects. BSFL are higher in calcium, and mealworms are higher in protein.

Which is better for Arowana, Oscar and Flowerhorn?
Many keepers prefer BSFL for the calcium, but both work as treats. Rotating them gives variety.

Can I feed them every day?
Both are high in fat, so feed them a few times a week alongside a good staple food.

Should I soak them first?
A short soak in tank water softens the larvae and helps prevent dry food from swelling in the gut.

How should I store them?
Keep the pack sealed in a cool, dry place away from sunlight.
