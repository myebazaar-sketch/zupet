What are almond leaves?

Almond leaves come from the Indian almond tree (Terminalia catappa). The leaves are dried and added to aquarium water, where they slowly release tannins. Tannins tint the water amber, which many keepers like because it looks like the natural streams and ponds where betta and many shrimp species live.

Why aquarium keepers use almond leaves

  • A natural look. The soft amber tint suits betta, shrimp and planted tanks.
  • A shelter and resting spot. Betta like to rest on and around leaves, and the leaves give cover to shy fish.
  • Food for shrimp. A soft biofilm grows on the leaf, and shrimp graze on it.
  • Breeding set-ups. Many betta keepers add a leaf to breeding and fry tanks.

Almond leaves are an aquarium accessory, not a medicine. They are not a substitute for clean water, a good filter and regular water changes.

How to use almond leaves in your tank

  1. Rinse. Rinse the leaf under clean water to remove dust.
  2. Soak. Soak it in hot (not boiling) water for 10–15 minutes so it sinks and starts to soften.
  3. Add. A common starting point is one leaf for every 10–20 litres of water. Start with fewer and add more if you want a deeper tint.
  4. Watch. Check your water and your fish. Shrimp tanks should start with a small piece.
  5. Replace. Replace the leaf every 2–4 weeks, or once it begins to break down. Remove any leftover pieces.

Almond leaves for betta and shrimp

Betta: Betta like calm, planted water. One leaf in a well-filtered tank gives them a place to rest and gives the water a natural tint. Watch your fish, and take the leaf out if you see any sign of stress.

Shrimp: Neocaridina and Caridina shrimp graze on the biofilm that grows on soaked leaves. Start with a small piece, and replace it before it turns to mush so it doesn't foul the water.

Tips for tanks in Gurgaon and Delhi NCR

  • Check your tap water. Tap water in Gurgaon and across Delhi NCR is often hard. Test your pH and hardness before you rely on leaves to change them, because the effect of a few leaves is small in hard water.
  • Use a dechlorinator for any water you add.
  • Summer heat: in hot months, leaves break down faster, so check them more often.
  • Store spare leaves in a dry, airy place away from damp.
